Introduction to Global South

What does Global South mean ?

The Global South is a term describing a broad grouping of countries, primarily from Africa, Asia, Latin America, and Oceania, that share a sense of underrepresentation in international systems. It is not a formal bloc nor strictly geographical, as it includes countries like China and India. Key groupings such as the G77 and the Non-Aligned Movement represent these nations as they assert themselves against a world order they believe was designed by and for Western powers. They argue that multilateral institutions like the UN, World Bank, and IMF benefit "only the selected few" and fail to adequately represent their interests.

These countries often bear a disproportionate burden from global issues—such as climate change, pandemics, and economic crises—that they did not create. Consequently, many are resisting pressure to align with major powers like the US, China, or Russia. However, the term "Global South" itself is contentious. Critics argue it is an overly simplistic label that masks the vast diversity among its members, from small island states to major economies, making unified action difficult and potentially creating a "barrier to understanding a complex world."

Women Artists from the Global South

Triple Marginalization

Women artists from the Global South face compounding struggles that go beyond what any single label can hold. Media from the Global North frequently portrays them through narrow, stereotypical lenses, exoticised figures, passive victims, denying their individuality and making it difficult for their authentic voices to be heard.The words “female,” “identities,” and “Global South” thus emerge as imposed labels that bring about what Goniwe calls the “burden of racial representation” an imposed identity that erases agency.

Their struggle is distinctly gendered: on top of economic and political hardships shared with men in their regions, women face additional systemic barriers — disproportionate unpaid care burdens, legal inequities, and limited access to resources — that compound into a cycle of constrained opportunity. Ultimately, supporting their voice and leadership is fundamental to any genuine understanding of global art.

Representation in Institutions

Representation through data

Our data from Videomuseum — a network of French museums and institutions managing collections of modern and contemporary art, including national, regional, departmental, municipal museums, Cnap, Frac, and foundations — documents 3485 artists.
Women artists make up less than 1 in 4 of all recorded artists. Women from the Global South represent only 1 in 10 artists total, and 44% of all women artists (343 out of 782).

How do they define Global South

Elodie Brun

I don't think the Global South as a concept is useless. It is a matter of defining it well, on the other hand, and of knowing how we want to use it.

In my opinion, it is a relevant tool to analyze critical political postures that promote solidarity between people who share a similar situation of oppression and inequality.

Maria Teresa

The category 'South' names connections or similarities between locations in the South. This includes, for example, shared histories of imperial domination or extraction; histories of political activism and solidarity; and also long histories of cultural exchange.

For me, these connections—which require a dynamic understanding of transregional histories—are the keystone of the Global South as a concept.
